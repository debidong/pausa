Rhythm & the 90-minute hard line

Rigid timers get quit on the first busy afternoon; infinitely snoozable timers get ignored all day. Pausa pairs a light 20-minute cadence with a finite snooze budget and one non-negotiable ceiling.

Short break 20sec / 20 min A soft blurred veil across every connected display. Click-through so nothing underneath loses focus, wiping itself away left to right as the 20 seconds elapse.
Long break 5min / 60 min Every third break opens a painted landscape—or your own photo, measured and dimmed automatically so white text stays legible. Snooze 5 or 15 minutes while your budget lasts.
Hard line 90min ceiling Even when calls or snoozes push breaks back, continuous screen time stops at 90 minutes. Past the hard line, snooze and Space-to-skip step aside until you take the rest.
  • Heads-up before anything covers your screen A quiet top card and a small countdown pill near the pointer appear seconds before a break, so you can finish a thought or snooze without breaking stride.
  • Waits until you stop typing or dragging If a break arrives mid-sentence or mid-drag, Pausa holds up to two minutes for a natural pause in keyboard and mouse input before fading in.
  • Dims after dark and nudges you to bed Scenes soften at 18:00. After 23:00, the break screen drops to low-luminance midnight glass and switches its prompts to gentle reminders to close the lid.
  • 30-day local rhythm log Work spans and completed breaks stay strictly on your Mac. Breaks held automatically for meetings or videos never count against you as skipped.

Zero-permission auto-pause

Pausa requests zero macOS TCC permissions—no Screen Recording, no Accessibility, no Input Monitoring, and zero network telemetry. It reads public system state and shows the exact reason right in the menu bar.

Install & specifications

Pausa is distributed directly as a universal disk image—free to use at home or at work, with no account and no background daemon.

  1. 01 Download Pausa for macOS (.dmg) and drag Pausa to Applications.
  2. 02 Open Pausa. macOS blocks the first launch because the build is ad-hoc signed rather than notarized under a paid Apple Developer certificate.
  3. 03 Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll to Security, and click Open Anyway. That is the only time you need to do this—subsequent updates verify via EdDSA-signed Sparkle feeds and install after breaks finish.
